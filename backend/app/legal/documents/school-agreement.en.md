# School Agreement

**Version 1.0 · in force from 17 September 2026**

This agreement is between Equip and **your school** — the organisation whose
courses, teachers and students live on this platform. You are being asked to
accept it because you are its director, and accepting it binds the school, not
only you personally. If you are not authorised to agree on the school's behalf,
stop here and tell us at supportequip@gmail.com.

It is in addition to the [Terms of Use](/terms), the
[Privacy Policy](/privacy) and the
[Teacher & Contributor Agreement](/teacher-terms). Where this document says
something more specific about the school, this document wins.

**Why it exists.** Until now the platform's other documents put obligations on
your school — parental consent, the conduct of your teachers, the truth of your
certificates — and your school had signed nothing. An obligation nobody accepted
is not an obligation. This is the school accepting them.

## 1. What your school is responsible for

Your school runs a school. We run software. Everything below follows from that.

**Your people.** Every account your school creates or invites — students,
teachers, administrators — is your school's responsibility: that the person is
who you say they are, that they belong in the course you put them in, and that
they know what they have been signed up for.

**Every notice and permission the law requires.** Your school gives the
notices and obtains the consents that apply where your school is, to the
students it enrols and to the people responsible for them. That includes
consent to a minor's enrolment, to their coursework being marked and stored, and
to their name being printed on a certificate that anybody holding its number can
look up. We do not have the relationships to do this and will not pretend
otherwise: you know the families and we do not.

**Telling the truth to us.** If your school loses the right to enrol somebody —
a parent withdraws consent, a student leaves, a teacher is dismissed — your
school removes their access promptly and tells us if anything needs deleting.

## 2. Age

**Your school will not create or invite an account for anybody under 13.** Not
in any circumstance, not with a parent's permission, not with a shared family
email address, not on a school device. The platform's floor is thirteen and it
is absolute.

Between **13 and 16**, only your school can open an account, and by doing so
your school confirms it holds the consent of whoever is responsible for that
student and can produce it if asked. From **16** a student can register
themselves.

If an account here turns out to belong to a child under 13, we close it and
delete its data, and we tell you. Repeated breaches of this section end this
agreement.

## 3. Material your teachers upload

The [Teacher & Contributor Agreement](/teacher-terms) makes each teacher
responsible for having the right to everything they upload. This section makes
your school responsible too, alongside them.

**Your school stands behind what its teachers publish.** Your school confirms
that material uploaded for its courses is the school's, licensed to it, in the
public domain, or otherwise free to share — and that scanned books, files from
file-sharing sites, another institution's curriculum and unlicensed translations
are not on your platform pages.

**Your school covers us if that material causes a claim.** If a claim is brought
against Equip, or against the people behind it, because of material uploaded for
your school's courses, because of how your school or its teachers handled
student data, or because your school broke this agreement, **your school will
defend us against that claim and cover what it costs us**, including reasonable
legal fees and any settlement or award. We will tell your school about the claim
promptly, let it run the defence if it wants to, and not settle in its name
without asking.

This is the same promise your teachers make individually. It is repeated at the
school level because a claim over a course is usually a claim about an
institution, and because a teacher who has left is a poor place for it to land.

## 4. Marks, certificates and what they say

**Your school's marks are your school's.** We record them, calculate from them,
print them and freeze them. We do not set them, review them, or vouch for them.

**Your school's certificates are your school's.** The decision to award one is
made by your school's people, in your school's name, on your school's letterhead.
An Equip certificate says a course was completed at your school. It does not say
anything about accreditation, and Equip is not an accredited institution.

**What we promise about them.** That the document is a faithful record of what
your school decided and when, frozen so that it cannot be quietly rewritten
afterwards — including if your school later changes its name.

**What your school promises about them.** That they are earned. A certificate
issued for work that was not done is your school's misrepresentation, not ours,
and it is grounds for us to end this agreement.

## 5. Data protection: who is who

For your students' coursework, marks, progress and submissions:

- **your school decides** what is collected, why, and who sees it — in
  data-protection language, your school is the controller;
- **we act on your school's instructions** — we are the processor. We process
  that data to run the platform for your school and for nothing else: we do not
  sell it, do not use it for advertising, and do not train models on it.

For accounts, sign-in, security and keeping the platform working, we decide, and
there we are the controller. The [Privacy Policy](/privacy) describes both
halves.

**Our sub-processors** — the infrastructure the platform cannot run without —
are listed in the annex to that policy, at
[/privacy/providers](/privacy/providers). Your school agrees to our using them.
When that list changes we publish it and tell you **60 days before it takes
effect**, so your school can ask about a new one, object, or leave.

**If something goes wrong.** If we learn of a breach affecting your students'
data, we tell your school without undue delay, with what we know and what we are
doing. We ask the same of your school in the other direction — including when
the cause was a teacher's own device or account.

**What we will give you.** At your school's request we will help it answer a
student's request about their own data, hand over an export of what your school
holds here, and delete what your school asks us to delete, subject to the
certificates and grade sheets that are frozen by design.

## 6. Parents asking for a child's records

A parent or guardian of a minor student at a private school in Indiana can ask
to see the records the school keeps about their child, and comparable rights
exist in other places.

**Answering that is your school's job**, because the records are your school's
and the relationship is your school's. **Giving your school the means is ours**:
your school can see and export everything the platform holds about one of its
students, and where the tooling does not go far enough, write to
supportequip@gmail.com and we will produce it by hand.

We will not hand a child's records to somebody claiming to be a parent on our
own judgement. We do not know your families; you do.

## 7. Your school's own paperwork

Some schools and districts have a standard agreement they ask every supplier to
sign — a National Data Privacy Agreement, a vendor addendum, a security rider.

**Sending us one does not make it binding.** No document your school supplies,
countersigns, attaches to a purchase order or links in an email becomes part of
this agreement unless we have agreed to it **in writing, specifically, by
name**. Silence is not acceptance, and neither is our continuing to provide the
platform.

This is not obstruction, and it is worth being open about the reason. Those
agreements routinely move disputes to the school's own state, require breach
notification inside 72 hours, require an annual third-party security audit, and
remove any cap on liability. Equip is one person and a server bill. Accepting
terms it cannot meet would be a worse answer to your school than declining them
plainly — so if your school needs something specific, write to us and we will
tell you honestly whether we can do it.

## 8. What Equip does not promise

The **no warranties** and **limits of liability** sections of the
[Terms of Use](/terms) apply to this agreement in full, including the cap. The
platform is provided as it is, to your school as to everybody else.

We do not promise the platform is fit for any accreditation, inspection or
regulatory regime your school is subject to. If your school must meet a standard
— an accreditor's, a government's, a denomination's — checking that this
platform lets it is your school's job, before it relies on it.

## 9. Ending this

**Your school can leave at any time**, by writing to supportequip@gmail.com.
**We can end this** if your school breaks this agreement, and — for anything
other than the age rule or a false certificate — we will say what is wrong and
give your school 30 days to put it right first.

When it ends:

- **Students keep their certificates.** Already issued, still checkable at the
  verification link, unchanged.
- **Students keep their own coursework** on their own accounts, and their
  accounts survive your school's departure.
- **Your school keeps its courses** in the sense that matters: before anything
  is removed we will give your school an export of its course material and its
  grade sheets. Ask for it; we will not delete a school's work as a punishment.
- **Students mid-course** are the thing we will not be brisk about. We will
  agree a wind-down with your school rather than cut a cohort off in week six.
- The sections meant to outlive it — your school's promises about material and
  data, the indemnity, the limits of liability, and the dispute terms in the
  Terms of Use — keep applying.

## 10. Changes to this agreement

The same rule as the other documents. A **material change** — one that adds an
obligation on your school, changes the indemnity, or changes the dispute terms —
asks your school to accept it again. Anything else is published, announced to
your school inside the platform, and **takes effect 60 days later**.

## 11. Disputes, law and the rest

The **Disputes** section of the [Terms of Use](/terms) applies to this
agreement: write first and wait 30 days, Indiana law, the courts of Indiana
exclusively. So do *Assignment*, *Notices*, *Force majeure*, *Severability*,
*Waiver*, *Entire agreement* and the other general terms in *The rest*.

Your school is agreeing to this as an organisation, not as an employee of one:
the person who clicks accept confirms they are authorised to bind it.

## 12. Languages

This agreement is published in English, Russian, German and Ukrainian. **If they
differ, the English version governs** — except where the law of the country the
school is in says otherwise.
